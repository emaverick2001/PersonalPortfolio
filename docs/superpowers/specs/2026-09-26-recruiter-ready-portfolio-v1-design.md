# Recruiter-Ready Portfolio v1 Design

Date: 2026-09-26
Status: Approved 2026-09-26

## Decision

Complete and release a recruiter-ready portfolio before expanding Daily Focus Coach into a full interactive case study. Daily Focus Coach is the next major product project and the first post-launch flagship expansion; it is not a dependency for Portfolio v1.

The release should lead with credible engineering evidence while preserving Maverick's interdisciplinary identity across AI, data, research systems, visualization, music technology, and human-centered tools.

## Goal

Make the portfolio useful in interviews and networking by giving a visitor a fast path from positioning to proof:

1. Understand what Maverick builds and why within about 10 seconds.
2. Reach a flagship project within one click from the homepage.
3. Understand the project's question, Maverick's contribution, system, evidence, limitations, and next action within 60–90 seconds.
4. Inspect authentic supporting evidence through a repository, visual artifact, demonstration, or clearly sourced result.
5. Reach a current resume and contact path without searching.

"Complete" means every public page and project is intentional, current enough to publish, and supported at the level claimed. It does not mean publishing every past project or implementing every future feature.

## Audience and positioning

The initial release should be credible for AI/ML, data, and software-engineering conversations, with interactive and creative systems as a memorable differentiator. The language must remain broad enough for interdisciplinary product and design-engineering conversations without presenting an unsupported seniority, specialty, or career identity.

Recommended homepage positioning:

> I build AI, data, and interactive systems that make complex problems easier to understand and act on.

This is a messaging direction, not immutable final copy. The final sentence should be reviewed alongside the updated resume so terminology remains consistent with the evidence.

## Release information architecture

### Homepage

Preserve the approved botanical design, night-first presentation, reversible seed-to-plant scroll sequence, restrained editorial typography, and human-flourishing theme.

Sharpen the first screen around four pieces of information:

- a specific professional positioning line;
- one plain-language sentence describing the kind of problems Maverick works on;
- a primary action to the strongest technical case study;
- secondary actions to Work and Resume.

The existing Daily Focus Coach section remains an honest "in development" direction. Its local perspective-shift interaction may remain as an illustrative prototype, but it must not be described as a validated product, trial result, persistent Coach, or live personal-data system.

### Work index

Organize Work into two tiers:

1. **Featured case studies:** complete, evidence-backed stories with their own routes.
2. **Selected work in progress:** short, clearly labeled entries with only verified role, purpose, status, and available evidence.

Do not display a large undifferentiated project archive. Do not expose generated project pages containing placeholder instructions, speculative roadmaps, unverified ownership, or generic repository prose.

The Work page should let a visitor compare featured projects quickly through consistent fields:

- question;
- domain;
- role;
- status;
- evidence available;
- one-sentence result or learning.

### Supporting pages

About, Research, Background, Personal, Music, Blog, Inspirations, and Resume remain supporting evidence for the broader person and practice. They receive a factual currency and link pass, not another visual redesign. Public copy should remain concise and remove internal planning language, stale status claims, placeholder content, and redundant promotional endings.

## Featured case studies

### 1. Molecule Generation with Reinforcement Learning

This is the primary technical case study for Portfolio v1.

Preserve its central strength: the implementation worked as a system, one seed improved, another did not reproduce the result, and the case study treats that instability as evidence rather than hiding it.

Add or refine a compact opening overview containing:

- research question;
- individual contribution;
- six-step system summary;
- strongest result and non-reproduction;
- evidence status and limitation;
- links to the repository and deeper explanation.

The existing pipeline and seed comparison can satisfy the minimum visual-evidence requirement if their labels, numbers, source basis, accessibility text, and mobile behavior pass review. No benchmark, scientific, or model-performance claim may extend beyond the repository and available experiment record.

### 2. Pure Data Synthesizer + Visualizer

This is the interdisciplinary flagship demonstrating interaction design, modular implementation, music technology, and visual thinking.

Keep the evidence-backed explanation of MIDI input, synthesis/effects, arpeggiation, GEM visuals, shared mappings, unfinished mappings, and scope narrowing from the original vocoder idea.

Before release, pair the existing system diagram and source references with at least one authentic visual artifact from the working project, preferably:

1. a short screen recording or silent looping clip showing the actual visual response;
2. a verified screenshot of the patch and visual output; or
3. if neither can be produced honestly, a clearly labeled source-based walkthrough that does not imply a live demo.

Do not fabricate screenshots, reconstruct undocumented output, or call the website's browser interaction a port of the Pure Data project.

### Selected work in progress

- **RevoStep:** keep a concise entry only if the dashboard contribution, status, and shareable evidence are confirmed. Do not claim ownership of the assistive device or publish protected team data.
- **Daily Focus Coach:** present as the next product project and an in-development direction. The current homepage interaction is illustrative, not a flagship case study.
- **Other projects:** include only after a source and contribution review. Unverified legacy entries remain preserved in repository history or internal source, but are not public routes in Portfolio v1.

## Fast project comprehension

Each featured case study begins with a reusable "quick tour" rather than forcing a recruiter to read the whole page. It is a concise summary region, not a carousel or mandatory animation. The visitor can scan it, follow a supporting link, or continue into the longer story.

The quick tour uses this order:

1. **Question:** What was worth investigating?
2. **Contribution:** What did Maverick personally design, build, test, or analyze?
3. **System:** How do the important parts connect?
4. **Evidence:** What happened, and where can it be inspected?
5. **Friction:** What failed, remained uncertain, or constrained the work?
6. **Next action:** What would be tested or built next?

This structure should also support a 30–90-second interview explanation without requiring the visitor to understand specialized terminology first.

## Resume and content currency

The September 20, 2025 resume is not sufficient for the final Portfolio v1 currency gate. Maverick will provide the current source resume or resumes. The portfolio will publish one reviewed, accurately dated primary resume first; role-specific AI Engineer, Data Engineer, or other variants remain hidden until separately prepared and verified.

Before staging, perform a factual review of:

- current resume dates, roles, education, skills, and project references;
- homepage positioning and "currently building" language;
- About, Background, Research, and Personal claims;
- project names, statuses, individual contributions, results, and links;
- contact and social destinations;
- blog, music, and inspiration links that appear in public navigation.

Unknown or private information should be omitted rather than inferred. Old status statements should not be converted into current claims without confirmation.

## Repository and route cleanup

Cleanup is evidence-driven and reversible:

- inventory existing public project routes, referenced assets, and source ownership;
- define an explicit allowlist for released project routes;
- stop generating stale or placeholder legacy project detail routes;
- remove legacy routes from sitemaps and navigation;
- preserve uncertain source material until its ownership and future value are reviewed;
- archive or delete assets only after proving they are unreferenced and receiving the applicable approval;
- verify that private portrait studies, temporary captures, downloaded handoffs, and local diagnostics are absent from build output and Git.

Do not perform broad deletion merely to make the repository look smaller. A recruiter-facing repository should instead show clear setup instructions, deterministic dependencies, accurate descriptions, no secrets, and an understandable relationship between the site and showcased projects.

## Visual and interaction boundary

Portfolio v1 preserves the approved warm ivory/forest green botanical language and does not add visual novelty solely for spectacle.

Allowed finishing work:

- authentic project media;
- concise diagrams that materially explain a system or result;
- responsive, accessible progressive disclosure;
- fixes for alignment, clipping, image quality, keyboard behavior, reduced motion, and performance.

Deferred:

- generated headshot integration;
- decorative 3D or optical-illusion scenes;
- travel globe and personal metrics dashboard;
- background music or narrated site journeys;
- interactive learning modules;
- public personal-media analytics;
- resume variants;
- semantic KV-cache case study until its evidence is reviewed.

## Daily Focus Coach and personal-media direction

After Portfolio v1 is released, Daily Focus Coach becomes the next foreground product. Its minimum harness should prove one permissioned information loop before connecting many sources:

1. ingest from one approved source;
2. retain provenance and public/private classification;
3. show what information influenced the response;
4. support one useful decision or action;
5. record an inspectable result, friction, and next action.

The longer-term architecture may connect personal media and activity sources through separate permissioned connectors, a private normalized event layer, the Coach interface, and later analytics or AI experiments. Raw personal data, credentials, private media, and durable Coach memory remain private by default. Only deliberately curated, aggregate, de-identified, or synthetic outputs may become portfolio demonstrations.

This direction is recorded so Portfolio v1 can anticipate the future story. It does not authorize connector development, account automation, ingestion, analytics, a personal-data schema, or Daily Focus Coach implementation in the current portfolio slice.

## Release sequence

1. **Resume intake and truth audit:** receive the current resume source, reconcile factual content, and identify unsupported public claims.
2. **Positioning and route curation:** sharpen the homepage and define the released project-route allowlist.
3. **Featured-project completion:** add the quick tours, verify the molecule evidence, and add authentic synthesizer visual evidence or explicitly document its absence.
4. **Supporting-content pass:** verify public copy, links, navigation, and project statuses without redesigning supporting pages.
5. **Targeted cleanup and local QA:** remove public stale output, verify repository hygiene, and run desktop/mobile/accessibility/privacy checks.
6. **Release foundation:** complete deterministic dependencies, environment boundaries, sitemap correction, and manual release workflows.
7. **Authenticated staging:** review the exact candidate on private hosted staging.
8. **Production release:** promote the identical reviewed SHA only after explicit approval.
9. **Next flagship:** begin Daily Focus Coach as a new product and future case-study cycle.

## Portfolio v1 completion gates

Portfolio v1 is ready for authenticated staging only when:

- one reviewed current resume is available;
- the homepage states a clear, evidence-compatible professional position;
- the molecule and synthesizer case studies pass the quick-comprehension and evidence checks;
- every public project route is explicitly allowlisted and reviewed;
- RevoStep and Daily Focus Coach are labeled at their actual status;
- no public placeholder, invented fact, stale status claim, broken destination, or private review artifact remains;
- the supported build, tests, type checking, accessibility checks, and desktop/mobile browser QA pass;
- analytics are disabled for the initial release;
- authenticated staging is configured to prevent indexing and unauthorized access; and
- the production workflow can promote only the exact staged SHA through a separate approval.

## Alternatives considered

### Release the current candidate immediately

Fastest, but it would preserve a dated resume and leave stale generated project material accessible. This does not meet the stated goal of current, comprehensive, interview-ready presentation.

### Publish an exhaustive project archive

More comprehensive by count, but weaker in trust and comprehension. Unsupported detail, incomplete projects, and repetitive templates make the strongest work harder to find.

### Wait for the full Daily Focus Coach flagship

Potentially the strongest future story, but it would keep the portfolio unfinished while a separate product, data boundary, and trial system are built. Daily Focus Coach is therefore the first post-launch flagship rather than a release prerequisite.

## Non-goals

- Building or validating Daily Focus Coach.
- Connecting personal media accounts or ingesting personal activity data.
- Adding production analytics, session replay, visitor-input capture, or durable visitor storage.
- Creating resume variants before the primary resume is current.
- Inventing project outcomes, reconstructing missing visual evidence, or presenting team work as individual work.
- Deploying, publishing, pushing, merging, or changing external accounts during design and planning.
- Redesigning the approved botanical visual system.
